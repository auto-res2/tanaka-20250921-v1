2. Baselines: DPO (β∈{0.1…10}×8); DiscoPOP (β∈{0.05…5}×8); PPO (KL-target κ∈{0.5,1,2,5}); SA-DPO (single run, PID tuned once by the original paper).

Expected results:
• C3PO’s single run matches or exceeds the best grid point of every baseline on reward (≥+2 % averaged) while respecting KL≤κ_max.
• Average GPU hours per task: 8 h (C3PO) vs. ~40–60 h (full sweeps) → 5–7× compute saving.
Demonstration: Verifies that “zero hyper-parameter search” is real and that the dual-ascent mechanism reliably finds the optimum, fulfilling Claims 1 & 3.

Experiment 2 – “Context awareness & safety sensitivity”
Objective: Confirm that the learned β_i adapts to input risk and difficulty, tightening the KL leash on toxic/OOD prompts and loosening it on easy in-distribution ones.
Design:
1. Fine-tune Mistral-7 B with C3PO on Anthropic Harmless for 2 epochs.  Log β_i, reward, KL per sample.
2. Create four evaluation splits (1000 samples each):
   A. Benign in-distribution queries.
   B. Long, high-entropy queries (length > P90).
   C. Adversarial jail-break prompts (AdvBench-Instruct).
   D. Held-out domain (medical Q&A).
3. Compute per-split statistics: median β_i, σ_KL, reward, harmlessness score (RLHF-Harmless model).
4. Correlate β_i with proxy features (msg length, reward-model entropy, toxicity score) via Spearman-ρ.
5. Ablation: freeze β network to global mean after epoch 1; continue training for another epoch and measure harmlessness drop.
Expected results:
• Median β_i: A≈0.7; B≈1.2; C≈3.8; D≈2.5.
• Strong positive correlation β_i↔toxicity (ρ≈0.6) and ↔RM-entropy (ρ≈0.5).

Demonstration: Validates ingredient 2 (context-aware network) and shows safety benefits (Open Problem 2).

Experiment 3 – “Trust-region & stability stress test”
Objective: Empirically check the monotone convergence guarantee and robustness to non-stationary rewards.

1. Dialogue setting (UltraFeedback, Pythia-1.4 B).  Record KL_t and dual variable β̄_t every 50 steps.

• Fixed-β: KL drifts, violates constraint permanently, reward collapses.

============================================
1. Goal

   • Reddit TL;DR (summarisation, 2020 cleaned split)  
   • IMDb Positive Steering (sentiment re-writing, “IMDb-P” split)  
   • Anthropic Harmless (safety, v0.5)

4. Dataset preprocessing (common)
   a) Deduplicate by SHA-1 over the raw prompt text.
   b) Filter length 4–512 tokens (sentencepiece of base model).
   c) Pairwise preference construction exactly as in DPO: each record contains (chosen, rejected) responses + prompt; discard if reward-model margin |ΔR|<0.05 to avoid label noise.
   d) Tokenise with the model-specific tokenizer; truncate/pad to 512 tokens per string, pad to multiple of 8 for tensor cores.

5. Data split
   • 80 % train / 10 % validation / 10 % hold-out test.
   • Same random seed (2023) for all methods to guarantee identical splits.

6. Baselines & grids
   a) DPO: β ∈ {0.1,0.2,0.4,0.8,1.6,3.2,6.4,10} (8 runs)  
   b) DiscoPOP: β ∈ {0.05,0.1,0.2,0.5,1,2,3,5} (8 runs)  
   c) PPO: KL-target κ ∈ {0.5,1,2,5} (4 runs)  
   d) SA-DPO (single PID run, gains from original paper †).

7. Optimiser & schedule (all methods)
   • AdamW, lr=1 e-5, β₁=0.9, β₂=0.95, weight-decay 0.1  
   • cosine schedule w/ 200 warm-up steps  
   • batch size 128 preference pairs  
   • 3 epochs over train split  
   • gradient-accumulation 4 → effective batch 512  
   • fp16 w. bfloat16 accumulate (Ampere).

8. Seeds & repetition
   • Seeds {42, 1234, 2023} (3 independent runs).   
   • Report mean±95 %CI over seeds.   
   • For each run store checkpoints each 500 optimisation steps.   
   • Selection: “best-val” (highest validation reward) for final evaluation; also log last-epoch numbers to measure over-fit.

9. Evaluation metrics
   Primary:  
   • Normalised reward (scale-matched to original RMs, higher=better).  
   • Mean KL(π||π₀) on validation.  
   Secondary:  
   • ROUGE-L (TL;DR), sentiment accuracy (IMDb-P), MT-Bench overall (dialogue), Harmlessness rate (Anthropic safety classifier).  
   • Runtime metrics: GPU-hours, wall-clock seconds per 1 k steps, peak VRAM.  
   • Energy (J) via nvidia-smi –query-accounted.

10. Hyper-parameter analysis
    • For C3PO sweep η_dual ∈ {0.02, 0.05, 0.1} on AlpacaFarm only to confirm default choice, record its effect on convergence & KL.

11. Robustness

This suite is intended to provide \na controlled setting for performing scientific experiments. We also provide \n154 checkpoints per model: initial `step0`, 10 log-spaced checkpoints \n`step{1,2,4...512}`, and 143 evenly-spaced checkpoints from `step1000` to \n`step143000`. These checkpoints are hosted on Hugging Face as branches. Note \nthat branch `143000` corresponds exactly to the model checkpoint on the `main` \nbranch of each model.\n\nYou may also further fine-tune and adapt Pythia-1.4B for deployment, \nas long as your use is in accordance with the Apache 2.0 license. Pythia \nmodels work with the Hugging Face [Transformers \nLibrary](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/index). If you decide to use \npre-trained Pythia-1.4B as a basis for your fine-tuned model, please \nconduct your own risk and bias assessment. \n\n### Out-of-scope use\n\nThe Pythia Suite is **not** intended for deployment. It is not a in itself \na product and cannot be used for human-facing interactions. For example, \nthe model may generate harmful or offensive text. Please evaluate the risks\nassociated with your particular use case.\n\nPythia models are English-language only, and are not suitable for translation \nor generating text in other languages.\n\nPythia-1.4B has not been fine-tuned for downstream contexts in which \nlanguage models are commonly deployed, such as writing genre prose, \nor commercial chatbots. This means Pythia-1.4B will **not** \nrespond to a given prompt the way a product like ChatGPT does. This is because,\n unlike this model, ChatGPT was fine-tuned using methods such as Reinforcement \nLearning from Human Feedback (RLHF) to better “follow” human instructions.\n\n### Limitations and biases\n\nThe core functionality of a large language model is to take a string of text \nand predict the next token. The token used by the model need not produce the \nmost “accurate” text. Never rely on Pythia-1.4B to produce factually accurate \noutput.\n\nThis model was trained on [the Pile](https://pile.eleuther.ai/), a dataset \nknown to contain profanity and texts that are lewd or otherwise offensive. \nSee [Section 6 of the Pile paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.00027) for a \ndiscussion of documented biases with regards to gender, religion, and race. \nPythia-1.4B may produce socially unacceptable or undesirable text, *even if* \nthe prompt itself does not include anything explicitly offensive. \n\nIf you plan on using text generated through, for example, the Hosted Inference \nAPI, we recommend having a human curate the outputs of this language model \nbefore presenting it to other people. Please inform your audience that the \ntext was generated by Pythia-1.4B.\n\n### Quickstart\n\nPythia models can be loaded and used via the following code, demonstrated here \nfor the third `pythia-70m-deduped` checkpoint:\n\n```python\nfrom transformers import GPTNeoXForCausalLM, AutoTokenizer\n\nmodel = GPTNeoXForCausalLM.from_pretrained(\n  "EleutherAI/pythia-70m-deduped",\n  revision="step3000",\n  cache_dir="./pythia-70m-deduped/step3000",\n)\n\ntokenizer = AutoTokenizer.from_pretrained(\n  "EleutherAI/pythia-70m-deduped",\n  revision="step3000",\n  cache_dir="./pythia-70m-deduped/step3000",\n)\n\ninputs = tokenizer("Hello, I am", return_tensors="pt")\ntokens = model.generate(**inputs)\ntokenizer.decode(tokens[0])\n```\n\nRevision/branch `step143000` corresponds exactly to the model checkpoint on \nthe `main` branch of each model.<br>\nFor more information on how to use all Pythia models, see [documentation on \nGitHub](https://github.com/EleutherAI/pythia).\n\n## Training\n\n### Training data\n\n[The Pile](https://pile.eleuther.ai/) is a 825GiB general-purpose dataset in \nEnglish.
