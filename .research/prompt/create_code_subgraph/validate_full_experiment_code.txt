# Experimental Design

## Experiment Strategy
Experiment 1 – “One-shot tuning vs. exhaustive sweeps”
Objective: Show that C3PO attains the same or better reward/KL trade-off than the best manually-tuned baselines, while cutting wall-clock and GPU hours.
Design:
1. Tasks & models: Pythia-1.4 B and Mistral-7 B on AlpacaFarm, UltraFeedback, TL;DR, IMDb-positive, Anthropic Harmless.
2. Baselines: DPO (β∈{0.1…10}×8); DiscoPOP (β∈{0.05…5}×8); PPO (KL-target κ∈{0.5,1,2,5}); SA-DPO (single run, PID tuned once by the original paper).
3. For every (task, model) pair, run:  a) one C3PO training job (η=0.05, κ_init=0).
                                               b) the full grid for each baseline.  All other hyper-parameters identical (AdamW, batch=128, 3 epochs).
4. Metrics collected each epoch: validation reward, mean KL, compute (GPU-sec), energy (nvidia-smi), memory.
5. Stop when validation reward plateaus for 3 checkpoints (all methods).
Expected results:
• C3PO’s single run matches or exceeds the best grid point of every baseline on reward (≥+2 % averaged) while respecting KL≤κ_max.
• Average GPU hours per task: 8 h (C3PO) vs. ~40–60 h (full sweeps) → 5–7× compute saving.
Demonstration: Verifies that “zero hyper-parameter search” is real and that the dual-ascent mechanism reliably finds the optimum, fulfilling Claims 1 & 3.

Experiment 2 – “Context awareness & safety sensitivity”
Objective: Confirm that the learned β_i adapts to input risk and difficulty, tightening the KL leash on toxic/OOD prompts and loosening it on easy in-distribution ones.
Design:
1. Fine-tune Mistral-7 B with C3PO on Anthropic Harmless for 2 epochs.  Log β_i, reward, KL per sample.
2. Create four evaluation splits (1000 samples each):
   A. Benign in-distribution queries.
   B. Long, high-entropy queries (length > P90).
   C. Adversarial jail-break prompts (AdvBench-Instruct).
   D. Held-out domain (medical Q&A).
3. Compute per-split statistics: median β_i, σ_KL, reward, harmlessness score (RLHF-Harmless model).
4. Correlate β_i with proxy features (msg length, reward-model entropy, toxicity score) via Spearman-ρ.
5. Ablation: freeze β network to global mean after epoch 1; continue training for another epoch and measure harmlessness drop.
Expected results:
• Median β_i: A≈0.7; B≈1.2; C≈3.8; D≈2.5.
• Strong positive correlation β_i↔toxicity (ρ≈0.6) and ↔RM-entropy (ρ≈0.5).
• Freezing β_i raises average KL on splits C/D by >30 % and degrades harmlessness by 7 %.  Reward on split A falls slightly (over-regularisation), proving adaptivity is useful.
Demonstration: Validates ingredient 2 (context-aware network) and shows safety benefits (Open Problem 2).

Experiment 3 – “Trust-region & stability stress test”
Objective: Empirically check the monotone convergence guarantee and robustness to non-stationary rewards.
Design:
1. Dialogue setting (UltraFeedback, Pythia-1.4 B).  Record KL_t and dual variable β̄_t every 50 steps.
2. At step 3 000 introduce a sudden reward shift: multiply reward by 0.3 for 500 steps (simulates reward misspecification) then restore original scale.
3. Run three variants: (i) C3PO (dual-ascent), (ii) SA-DPO (PID), (iii) Fixed-β DPO tuned for best final reward.
4. Measure:  a) KL_t – κ_max over time (should stay ≤0).  b) Convergence speed to new optimum (steps to regain 95 % of pre-shift reward).  c) Oscillation amplitude of KL (std over 1 000-step window).
Expected results:
• C3PO: KL_t never exceeds κ_max; β̄_t adjusts smoothly; reward recovers in ≤400 steps.
• SA-DPO: transient KL spikes >1.4 κ_max; oscillation std≈0.3; recovery in ~900 steps.
• Fixed-β: KL drifts, violates constraint permanently, reward collapses.
Demonstration: Confirms theoretical guarantee (Open Problem 3) and shows superior stability under non-stationary conditions.

Collective evidence: The three experiments jointly showcase C3PO’s strengths – hyper-parameter-free efficiency, context-aware safety, and provably stable optimisation – thereby demonstrating the practical value of the proposed method.

4. Dataset preprocessing (common)
   a) Deduplicate by SHA-1 over the raw prompt text.
   b) Filter length 4–512 tokens (sentencepiece of base model).
   c) Pairwise preference construction exactly as in DPO: each record contains (chosen, rejected) responses + prompt; discard if reward-model margin |ΔR|<0.05 to avoid label noise.
   d) Tokenise with the model-specific tokenizer; truncate/pad to 512 tokens per string, pad to multiple of 8 for tensor cores.

5. Data split
   • 80 % train / 10 % validation / 10 % hold-out test.
   • Same random seed (2023) for all methods to guarantee identical splits.

6. Baselines & grids
   a) DPO: β ∈ {0.1,0.2,0.4,0.8,1.6,3.2,6.4,10} (8 runs)

   c) PPO: KL-target κ ∈ {0.5,1,2,5} (4 runs)  
   d) SA-DPO (single PID run, gains from original paper †).

7. Optimiser & schedule (all methods)
   • AdamW, lr=1 e-5, β₁=0.9, β₂=0.95, weight-decay 0.1  
   • cosine schedule w/ 200 warm-up steps  
   • batch size 128 preference pairs  
   • 3 epochs over train split  
   • gradient-accumulation 4 → effective batch 512  
   • fp16 w. bfloat16 accumulate (Ampere).

8. Seeds & repetition
   • Seeds {42, 1234, 2023} (3 independent runs).   
   • Report mean±95 %CI over seeds.   
   • For each run store checkpoints each 500 optimisation steps.

This suite is intended to provide \na controlled setting for performing scientific experiments. We also provide \n154 checkpoints per model: initial `step0`, 10 log-spaced checkpoints \n`step{1,2,4...512}`, and 143 evenly-spaced checkpoints from `step1000` to \n`step143000`. These checkpoints are hosted on Hugging Face as branches. Note \nthat branch `143000` corresponds exactly to the model checkpoint on the `main` \nbranch of each model.\n\nYou may also further fine-tune and adapt Pythia-1.4B for deployment, \nas long as your use is in accordance with the Apache 2.0 license. Pythia \nmodels work with the Hugging Face [Transformers \nLibrary](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/index). If you decide to use \npre-trained Pythia-1.4B as a basis for your fine-tuned model, please \nconduct your own risk and bias assessment. \n\n### Out-of-scope use\n\nThe Pythia Suite is **not** intended for deployment. It is not a in itself \na product and cannot be used for human-facing interactions. For example, \nthe model may generate harmful or offensive text. Please evaluate the risks\nassociated with your particular use case.\n\nPythia models are English-language only, and are not suitable for translation \nor generating text in other languages.\n\nPythia-1.4B has not been fine-tuned for downstream contexts in which \nlanguage models are commonly deployed, such as writing genre prose, \nor commercial chatbots. This means Pythia-1.4B will **not** \nrespond to a given prompt the way a product like ChatGPT does. This is because,\n unlike this model, ChatGPT was fine-tuned using methods such as Reinforcement \nLearning from Human Feedback (RLHF) to better “follow” human instructions.\n\n### Limitations and biases\n\nThe core functionality of a large language model is to take a string of text \nand predict the next token. The token used by the model need not produce the \nmost “accurate” text. Never rely on Pythia-1.4B to produce factually accurate \noutput.\n\nThis model was trained on [the Pile](https://pile.eleuther.ai/), a dataset \nknown to contain profanity and texts that are lewd or otherwise offensive. \nSee [Section 6 of the Pile paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.00027) for a \ndiscussion of documented biases with regards to gender, religion, and race. \nPythia-1.4B may produce socially unacceptable or undesirable text, *even if* \nthe prompt itself does not include anything explicitly offensive. \n\nIf you plan on using text generated through, for example, the Hosted Inference \nAPI, we recommend having a human curate the outputs of this language model \nbefore presenting it to other people.
