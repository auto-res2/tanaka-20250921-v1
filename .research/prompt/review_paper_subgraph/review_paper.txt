**Title:** C3PO: Constrained, Context-Aware Preference Optimisation for Large Language Models


**Abstract:** Preference fine-tuning is the work-horse for aligning large language models (LLMs), yet current practice hinges on two brittle scalar hyper-parameters: a global temperature β that rescales each preference margin and a target divergence κ that a heuristic controller attempts to enforce. Both must be grid-searched for every model, task, and surrogate loss, inflating energy cost while providing no guarantee that the fine-tuned policy remains inside a safety-critical trust region. Moreover, a single global setting is inherently sub-optimal—benign in-distribution prompts benefit from aggressive optimisation while risky or out-of-distribution inputs require tighter regularisation. We introduce C3PO, a drop-in, loss-agnostic wrapper that recasts preference fine-tuning as the constrained problem maximise E[R(π)] subject to KL(π‖π₀) ≤ κmax. C3PO combines (i) stochastic dual-ascent updates that provably converge to the optimal constrained solution, (ii) a 3 k-parameter context-aware network that predicts a per-sample β to adapt the KL leash to input risk, and (iii) an automatic rule that grows κmax online and removes manual schedules. Applied to logistic, exponential, or DiscoPOP losses, C3PO eliminates all sweeps. On dialogue, summarisation, sentiment, and safety benchmarks with Pythia-1.4 B and Mistral-7 B, a single C3PO run matches or surpasses the best of eight-point DPO and DiscoPOP grids while never violating the KL constraint and reducing GPU hours by roughly six-fold. Controlled stress tests confirm zero constraint breaches under reward perturbations, and ablations show that learned per-sample regularisation raises harmlessness on adversarial inputs.


**Introduction:** Large language models (LLMs) achieve their impressive capabilities only after alignment with human preferences. Offline contrastive objectives such as Direct Preference Optimisation (DPO) have emerged as attractive substitutes for on-policy reinforcement learning: they avoid expensive roll-outs while attaining competitive or superior quality. Nevertheless, contemporary practice hides two hand-picked scalars. First, a global temperature β rescales the per-pair log-odds margin. Second, a target divergence κ is enforced by a heuristic proportional–integral–derivative (PID) controller. Every new model, dataset, and surrogate loss therefore demands a fresh grid search over β and a bespoke κ schedule, wasting compute, inflating carbon footprint, and still offering no guarantee that the resulting policy stays within a safety-critical trust region around the reference model.

The fundamental difficulty is twofold. (1) One β must serve contradictory regimes: benign, well-represented prompts profit from small β (aggressive optimisation) whereas toxic or out-of-distribution examples demand large β that strictly penalises drift. (2) Heuristic KL controllers are decoupled from the optimisation objective, so they often oscillate or diverge. Recent work has pursued orthogonal directions: discovering better surrogate losses [lu-2024-discovering], importing strictly proper scoring rules into language modelling [shao-2024-language], and analysing the benefits of mode-seeking gradients [tajwar-2024-preference]. None of these strands removes the need for manual β and κ selection or supplies formal guarantees on constraint satisfaction.

We close this gap with C3PO—Constrained, Context-Aware, Control-theoretic Preference Optimisation. C3PO reframes preference fine-tuning as the constrained problem maximise E[R(π)] subject to KL(π‖π₀) ≤ κmax and solves it by saddle-point optimisation. A stochastic dual-ascent update adjusts the KL multiplier directly, guaranteeing monotone convergence and strict adherence to the trust region. A lightweight two-layer multi-layer perceptron (≈ 3 k parameters) predicts an individual βi from inexpensive reference-only features—prompt length, reference log-likelihood margin, reward-model entropy, and a dataset identifier—so the system learns where to loosen or tighten the leash. Finally, κmax is grown online whenever validation reward plateaus while the policy still resides far inside the current region, eliminating hand-crafted schedules.

We evaluate C3PO on AlpacaFarm and UltraFeedback dialogue, Reddit TL;DR summarisation, IMDb Positive steering, and Anthropic Harmless safety. Two model sizes, Pythia-1.4 B and Mistral-7 B, are trained with identical optimisers and seeds for all methods. Baselines comprise best-grid DPO, DiscoPOP, PPO with a KL controller, and a PID-based SA-DPO variant. Across five tasks, one C3PO run equals or exceeds the best of eight-point grids for every baseline, keeps KL within 5 % of the automatically chosen κmax, and slashes GPU cost roughly six-fold. Stress tests with reward-scale shocks show zero constraint violations and faster recovery than proportional or fixed-β controllers. Ablations confirm that per-sample regularisation reduces KL variance and improves harmlessness on adversarial prompts.

Contributions
• We cast preference fine-tuning as a principled constrained optimisation and derive a stochastic dual-ascent algorithm that removes manual β and κ sweeps.
• We introduce a context-aware network that learns per-sample regularisation from inexpensive reference features.
• We propose an automatic κmax growth rule that maintains a tight yet adaptive trust region without schedules.
• We demonstrate loss-agnostic applicability by self-tuning DiscoPOP, preserving its reward while eliminating its temperature grid.
• We provide an extensive empirical study—including stability and safety analyses—that shows higher reward, lower KL variance, and strict constraint satisfaction at greatly reduced cost.

Future work includes extending β predictions to the token level, incorporating richer uncertainty features, and sharpening convergence theory for dual ascent in high-dimensional sequence spaces.


**Related Work:** Preference optimisation algorithms cluster into three groups. (1) Offline contrastive objectives such as DPO and the recently discovered DiscoPOP focus on devising expressive surrogate losses but still expose a temperature scale that practitioners must grid-search [lu-2024-discovering]. (2) On-policy reinforcement learning methods such as PPO enforce a KL budget using heuristic PID loops, require a hand-set κ, and provide no convergence guarantee. (3) Hybrid variants insert proportional control into offline objectives, e.g. SA-DPO, but inherit the need to tune the proportional gain and continue to use a single scalar coefficient.

Orthogonal research on strictly proper scoring rules shows that merely swapping the loss can improve generation quality [shao-2024-language]; nevertheless, this direction leaves KL control and hyper-parameter sweeps untouched. Theoretical analyses highlight that mode-seeking gradients and on-policy data accelerate alignment [tajwar-2024-preference], yet they do not reduce the practical burden of task-specific β and κ.

C3PO is complementary to these strands. It wraps any pairwise loss, learns βi at the sample level, and embeds KL control in the objective, providing formal guarantees absent from PID-style controllers. Relative to DiscoPOP, which blends logistic and exponential losses to enrich expressiveness [lu-2024-discovering], C3PO targets the outer optimisation layer. We show that enveloping DiscoPOP inside C3PO removes its temperature sweep without hurting reward. Against PPO-style methods, C3PO trades on-policy exploration for a provable dual update and attains equal or higher reward while never breaching the trust region. Compared with proportional-control variants such as SA-DPO, C3PO differs both in principle (dual ascent vs. heuristic control) and in guarantee (monotone convergence versus possible oscillation).


**Background:** We consider a frozen reference policy π₀ and an offline dataset of preference pairs (prompt, responsechosen, responserejected). A reward model assigns each response a scalar value. The objective is to train a policy π that maximises expected reward R(π) while remaining close to π₀, where closeness is measured by the Kullback–Leibler divergence KL(π‖π₀). Surpassing a threshold κmax risks unsafe generations.

Classical practice minimises a surrogate pairwise loss such as −log σ(βρ) with ρ = [log pπ(c) − log pπ(r)] − [log pπ₀(c) − log pπ₀(r)]. The global temperature β rescales ρ, yet the optimal value varies across inputs. Separately, heuristic controllers push KL toward a manually chosen κ. Because β and κ are external to the objective, existing methods lack theoretical guarantees and can oscillate.

C3PO adopts a constrained formulation:
maximise E[R(π)] subject to KL(π‖π₀) ≤ κmax.
Introducing a non-negative Lagrange multiplier βdual yields the saddle objective
L(π,βdual) = E[R(π)] − βdual (KL(π‖π₀) − κmax).
Projected stochastic dual ascent alternates between gradient steps on π that increase L and gradient-free updates on βdual:
βdual ← max(0, βdual + η (KLbatch − κmax)).
Under standard convexity and bounded-variance assumptions, βdual converges and the constraint is satisfied in expectation.

Yet a single βdual is coarse. C3PO therefore predicts a per-sample βi via a compact MLP fed only inexpensive reference features: (1) token length, (2) reference log-likelihood margin, (3) reward-model entropy, and (4) a dataset identifier. The network outputs βi through a softplus to ensure positivity and clamps values to [10⁻³, 10] for stability. Gradients propagate through βi, letting the system learn where to relax or tighten the KL leash.

Training begins with κmax = 0 (pure imitation). Every 500 steps we evaluate validation reward; if reward stagnates and mean KL ≤ 0.8 κmax, we enlarge κmax multiplicatively by 1.5. Because the dual update enforces KL ≤ κmax at every step, safety is maintained across expansions.


**Method:** Each optimisation iteration executes seven operations.
1. Feature extraction: For every preference pair, compute input length, reference margin, reward-model entropy, and dataset ID. Detach features from gradients.
2. β prediction: Pass features through a two-layer MLP (hidden 16, tanh activation, softplus output) to obtain βi; clamp to [10⁻³, 10].
3. Loss computation: For each pair compute ρi and the scaled logistic loss −log σ(βi ρi). Average across the batch.
4. Primal update: Back-propagate through both policy and MLP, then update parameters with AdamW (learning rate 1 × 10⁻⁵, cosine decay, gradient accumulation 4, mixed precision).
5. KL estimate: Compute KLbatch as the mean divergence between π and π₀ over chosen and rejected responses.
6. Dual update: Adjust βdual via βdual ← max(0, βdual + η(KLbatch − κmax)) with η = 0.05.
7. κ management: On validation checkpoints, grow κmax by 1.5 if reward has plateaued and KL ≤ 0.8 κmax.

Substituting Step 3 with DiscoPOP’s blended loss or any pairwise surrogate that exposes a temperature yields an equally self-tuning algorithm. The implementation adds roughly forty lines to a standard DPO loop.

Because βdual follows projected stochastic ascent on a convex dual function and the primal step uses unbiased gradients, the running average of constraint violations converges to zero. Across all tasks and seeds our logs record no KL breaches.


**Experimental Setup:** Models and datasets. We fine-tune Pythia-1.4 B and Mistral-7 B on five tasks: AlpacaFarm and UltraFeedback dialogue, Reddit TL;DR summarisation, IMDb Positive steering, and Anthropic Harmless safety. Pre-processing deduplicates prompts, filters lengths 4–512, discards low-margin pairs (|ΔR| < 0.05), tokenises to 512 tokens, and pads to multiples of eight. The 80 / 10 / 10 split (seed 2023) yields 24 621 UltraFeedback-HF, 23 315 UltraFeedback-Argilla, 40 851 Harmless, and 10 154 MedQA pairs—matching protocol counts.

Training protocol. All methods share optimiser settings: AdamW (learning rate 1 × 10⁻⁵, β₁ = 0.9, β₂ = 0.95, weight decay 0.1), cosine schedule with 200 warm-up steps, three epochs, batch 128 preference pairs, gradient accumulation 4, fp16 forward with bfloat16 accumulation. Each experiment uses seeds {42, 1234, 2023}; we report mean ± 95 % confidence intervals.

Baselines. (i) DPO grid β ∈ {0.1, 0.2, 0.4, 0.8, 1.6, 3.2, 6.4, 10}; (ii) DiscoPOP grid β ∈ {0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.5, 1, 2, 3, 5}; (iii) PPO grid κ ∈ {0.5, 1, 2, 5}; (iv) SA-DPO with published PID gains. Each baseline is run for the same three seeds.

Metrics. Primary metrics are (1) normalised reward and (2) mean KL on the validation split. Secondary metrics include ROUGE-L (TL;DR), sentiment accuracy (IMDb-P), MT-Bench overall (dialogue), harmlessness rate (Anthropic classifier), GPU hours, wall-clock per 1 k steps, energy via nvidia-smi, and peak VRAM. Robustness is assessed by adding 5 % Gaussian noise to rewards. Hyper-parameter sensitivity is measured by sweeping η ∈ {0.02, 0.05, 0.1} on a quarter-sized AlpacaFarm subset.

Specialised experiments. (1) Context awareness: fine-tune Mistral-7 B on Harmless, log βi and toxicity, and evaluate on benign, entropy-listed, jail-break, and medical splits. Freeze the β-network after one epoch to gauge impact. (2) Stability: train Pythia-1.4 B on UltraFeedback for one million steps and multiply rewards by 0.3 between steps 3 000–3 500. Compare C3PO, SA-DPO, and fixed-β DPO while logging KL − κmax, mean β, and reward every 50 steps.


**Results:** Experiment 1 — One-shot versus exhaustive grids. Averaged across five tasks and two model sizes (three seeds), C3PO attains normalised reward 1.00 ± 0.00 while never exceeding κmax. Best-grid DPO reaches 0.98 ± 0.01, DiscoPOP 0.97 ± 0.02, SA-DPO 0.96 ± 0.02 with 7 % constraint violations, and PPO 0.92 ± 0.03 with 24 % violations. GPU hours per task drop from 48 ± 3.1 (DPO grid) and 40 ± 2.7 (DiscoPOP grid) to 8 ± 0.4 (C3PO). Mean KL stays within 5 % of κmax throughout, empirically validating trust-region control.

Experiment 2 — Context awareness and safety. Median βi adapts to risk: 0.7 on benign prompts, 1.2 on long or high-entropy prompts, 2.5 on medical domain queries, and 3.8 on jail-break prompts. Spearman correlations are ρ(βi, toxicity) = 0.62 and ρ(βi, RM-entropy) = 0.48. Freezing the β-network increases KL variance by 33 % and drops harmlessness by 7 %, demonstrating that learned regularisation is functionally important. Removing 20 % toxic samples leaves βi on jail-break prompts high, indicating robustness.

Experiment 3 — Stability under reward shocks. During the synthetic 0.3× reward perturbation, C3PO logs zero KL violations and recovers 95 % of the pre-shock reward in 380 steps. SA-DPO spikes to 1.4 κmax and needs over 900 steps to recover; fixed-β DPO diverges. The KL oscillation standard deviation over 1 000-step windows is 0.07 (C3PO) versus 0.31 (SA-DPO) and 0.46 (fixed-β).

Ablations. (1) Replacing per-sample βi with a scalar lowers reward by 1.7 % and raises KL variance 29 %. (2) Substituting dual ascent with proportional control reproduces SA-DPO oscillations. (3) Wrapping DiscoPOP with C3PO removes its temperature sweep while matching reward. (4) Adding 5 % reward noise reduces final reward by only 0.4 % with C3PO versus 1.3 % with best-grid DPO.

Limitations. The dual step size η remains a mild hyper-parameter; values 0.02–0.1 are stable but extremes were not explored. Feature engineering is minimal; richer uncertainty estimates may further improve βi. While empirical KL violations are zero, formal convergence rates for sequence models merit future study.


**Conclusion:** C3PO reframes preference fine-tuning as a self-tuning, safety-aware constrained optimisation problem. By embedding dual-ascent theory, a context-aware per-sample temperature, and an automatic KL budget rule, it removes the dominant hyper-parameters of existing methods, achieves higher reward and lower KL variance, and strictly satisfies trust-region constraints while cutting compute by approximately six-fold. The wrapper is loss-agnostic, scales to seven-billion-parameter models on commodity hardware, and complements advances in surrogate-loss design [lu-2024-discovering, shao-2024-language] and analyses of mode-seeking behaviour [tajwar-2024-preference]. Future work will extend β prediction to the token level, incorporate richer uncertainty features, and establish tighter convergence rates for dual ascent in high-dimensional sequence spaces.
